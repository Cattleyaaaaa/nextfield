summary: "Not every capability needs a permanent header button. Keep core routes direct and make less frequent actions discoverable through clear navigation and settings."
---

A header is a difficult place to refuse another feature. Learning gets a button, messages and analytics get two more, and language, theme, effects, accounts and an assistant also need entries. Each addition seems reasonable until the row becomes a feature inventory.

Crowding concerns hierarchy as well as spacing. Smaller buttons or two rows accommodate more content without deciding which entries deserve constant prominence.

## Give one row priorities

A visitor typically identifies the site, finds content and then adjusts preferences or uses a specific tool. The header can follow that order: identity, core routes, a prominent action and less frequent settings.

Keeping Field Agent visible does not require every utility to be equally visible. Its direct entry can remain while language, effects, badges and account controls move into settings.

The brand already returns home, so another home button can be redundant. Removing duplication leaves room without removing capability.

## A menu needs organisation

Moving entries into a menu still requires design. Learning and deeper sections can live in Explore. Messages and analytics also deserve direct header entries for participation and inspection. Language and interaction preferences concern how the site is used. Give those groups recognisable entrances.

A “more” menu containing everything reduces header width while transferring the full interpretation cost elsewhere. The next layer should explain its structure rather than make someone guess.

Icon-only controls need readable accessible names. Expanded links should supply titles and useful descriptions. Removing persistent text requires providing it in an appropriate place.

## A narrow screen has its own layout

Desktop can show About, Projects and Writing directly. Phones can place those paths in Explore while keeping the brand, assistant and essential controls visible.

Menus themselves must respect viewport dimensions. A fixed desktop width can extend beyond smaller windows. Bounded width, height and scrolling keep the destinations reachable.

A single-row header also gives sticky page content a stable reference. It no longer needs to accommodate a navigation bar that sometimes wraps.

## Infrequent actions still matter

Language, accounts and motion preferences may be used less often while being essential at a particular moment. A clear settings button and predictable grouping preserve discovery.

Walk through actual tasks: read, change language, disable motion, enter learning, inspect the account and return. If each requires searching again, a tidier screenshot has not created an easier experience.

Navigation should shorten primary routes while keeping other routes understandable. Separating frequently used entries from the complete map can reduce permanent buttons without reducing the range of available actions.
