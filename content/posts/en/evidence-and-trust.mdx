title: "How Far Can Evidence Take Us?"
summary: "Showing the process can build trust, but it cannot replace a clear account of a conclusion's limits."

It is easy to write online about what we can do. Project screenshots, code snippets, and build records make those claims easier to check. But evidence is not a switch: having it does not erase every question.

## Evidence always has a scope

A screenshot can show that an interface existed at one point; it cannot show that the interface works on a slow device. A passing test can show that certain inputs produced the expected results; it cannot show that no error will ever occur. Each kind of evidence answers a different question.

So when presenting evidence, it helps to say what it **does not prove**. A demo environment is not production. Personal practice is not a team delivery. An automated score is not a certification of professional ability. Clearer boundaries help visitors decide whether the evidence is useful to them.

## The process is edited too

Even a public build log is curated. We choose which versions to keep, which mistakes to show, and which drafts to delete. “Complete transparency” is often impossible. It is more honest to explain the selection criteria and retain the context that matters.

## Trust comes from being correctable

When a record contains an error or outdated idea, updating it need not erase the earlier mistake. A sentence explaining why we thought that way before and why we changed our minds can say more than a perfectly rewritten story.

Evidence cannot make the final judgment for us. It can, however, give judgment a place to stand—and give others a chance to disagree.
