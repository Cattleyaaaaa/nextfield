When an idea first appears, we tend to ask what it is: a project, an article, an experiment, or a deliverable feature? That sounds like organizing, but it can also close a door.

## Classification often comes too soon

Naming has a cost: the moment you name something, you accept an existing definition. Put a vague thought under “project,” and it must suddenly answer questions about timeline, output, and acceptance criteria. Put it under “article,” and it needs an argument, evidence, and a conclusion. But perhaps it began as nothing more than “I noticed something, and I do not know how to describe it yet.”

I ran into this repeatedly while building this site:

- A layout adjustment began as a bug fix; writing about it revealed a principle about the coordinate system of decoration.
- A radio change began as adding cover art and ended up becoming three records from different angles.
- Deleted approaches felt like failures at first, but later explained how a judgment formed better than the successful result did.

None would remain if I had filtered them through “Does this count as a formal project?” at the beginning.

## Let it exist first

A growing website should have room for unfinished work, questions, and observations without conclusions. They do not need to pretend to be finished pieces. They only need a recognizable trace that can connect to something later. “Recognizable” has a standard:

1. **Say what it is.** A sentence should make that clear before someone reads to the end.
2. **Say why it is here.** Is it unfinished, abandoned, or simply not understood yet?
3. **Leave a way back in.** On returning, you should know where the thought stopped.

These matter more than completeness. A clearly marked unfinished note can be more useful than something forced into a weak conclusion.

## Different degrees of completion, different places

This site has several layers for work at different stages:

| Place | What belongs there | Permitted state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing | Considered, reusable long-form pieces | Complete |
| Notes | Short observations without final conclusions | In progress |
| Open Lab | Interaction research that is not yet a product | Free to dismantle |
| Failure Museum | Abandoned approaches | Keep the judgment |
| Build Log | What changed in each version and why | Running record |

The point is not how many layers there are. It is that **each layer permits a kind of unfinished work**. A site with only a “published” state pushes you to force clarity too soon—or throw the idea away.

## Naming can wait

The best moment to name an idea is often not its first appearance, but its second or third. By then you know whether it is a recurring kind of problem or merely a coincidence. Until then, give it a place and let it stay.
