---
title: "The Catalogue Is Part of Listening"
date: "2026-10-12"
tags: ["Catalogue", "Artwork", "Discovery"]
summary: "A playlist is more than a file list behind a player. What is playing, what comes next and where the listener came from should remain connected."
---

A player explains what is happening now; a catalogue shapes what might happen next. A long list of titles can turn choosing into a search task. A wall of artwork can hide the songs and artists behind attractive squares. A useful catalogue keeps browsing connected to listening.

## Artwork helps recognition

Familiar album art is often recognised before its title. Placing artwork beside every track lets a listener scan by colour and shape and return to something heard before. It cannot carry the whole description: title, artist and playback state must remain readable. A missing cover needs a consistent placeholder rather than a broken gap.

The active track does not need a large highlight that overwhelms the rest. A quiet border, a playing indicator and dependable text contrast can show where the sound comes from while leaving the other songs available to explore.

## Searching and returning are different journeys

Some listeners arrive with a title in mind. Others want to wander through an artist's work. Search and filtering can share one catalogue without making an artist choice a prerequisite for seeing any music. Start with the complete library, narrow it as the query changes, and restore the list when the query is cleared.

Another action is easy to miss: after exploring many other tracks, someone wants to find the song that is playing. The catalogue can show the current track prominently or mark its place in the list. Either way, scroll position should not become an obstacle. A list must actually respond to a mouse wheel, trackpad and touch; merely looking scrollable is not enough if an outer container catches the gesture.

## Opening a catalogue should not interrupt a song

Selecting another track changes the music. Opening the catalogue only means the listener wants to look. Audio can continue, playback controls can remain available, and closing the catalogue should return to the same scene and progress.

This is why the entrance belongs near the controls. The journey from listening to finding and back should be short. A catalogue is useful not because it can display many songs, but because someone can find the next one without losing the current moment.
