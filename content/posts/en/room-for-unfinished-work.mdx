Portfolios often make room only for finished things. A project needs a polished cover, a clear result, and a value proposition that fits in one sentence. Questions still being explored are left in private notes.

## Unfinished does not always mean failed

An experiment may answer only a small question: does a certain animation make text easier to read? Does a particular confirmation pattern help someone understand a risk? It is not yet a product, but the answer still has value. Calling it an “experiment” is more honest than dressing it up as a complete case study.

## Publishing changes how we think

When an idea is meant to be seen, we have to explain what it is now, what it is not, and what is still missing. That is not an early declaration of success. It leaves a version of the thought behind. Months later, the most interesting part is often not the original conclusion but why the question was worth asking.

Publishing, of course, does not mean dumping every draft. Fragments without context make readers do the author's organizing. A short title, a question, and a current status are enough to make unfinished work understandable.

## Leave an exit

An experiment can grow into a project or be abandoned. If abandoned, explain why, so it can inform the next decision. If continued, keep the older version so the change remains visible.

A growing website needs more than a display shelf. It needs a place where ideas can stop for a while.
