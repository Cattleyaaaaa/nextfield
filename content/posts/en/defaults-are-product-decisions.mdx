A product makes decisions before someone touches it. It selects a theme, an initial sound state, answer depth and enabled effects. Defaults shape the first experience rather than sit outside design as technical details.

Most visitors do not read the entire manual before adjusting preferences. The initial state must support an unprepared arrival and avoid making difficult-to-reverse choices on their behalf.

## Support first use

A newcomer may not know where sound comes from or how to disable animation. Quiet initial behaviour, clear navigation and a discoverable settings entry reduce that uncertainty.

Answer depth creates a similar balance. Too little makes a question feel barely considered; too much demands reading background before receiving the answer. Start with the point and develop it according to the question and evidence.

A default need not suit everyone. It should provide a dependable starting point and a simple way to change it. Evaluate both untouched behaviour and the path to adjustment.

## Respect later choices

An initial theme suggestion should give way after an explicit preference. Reapplying it on every return undermines the settings control's promise.

Persistence has scope too. Remembering volume does not require automatically initiating sound on a new visit. Retaining a preference and starting an action are different decisions.

Keeping a conversation when a panel closes supports exploration, while clearing it on refresh establishes a different persistence boundary. Both can be reasonable if the explanation does not imply permanent memory.

## Quotas are public rules

Daily caps, a site-wide limit and whether failures count affect how a visitor uses the assistant. Revealing them only through an error makes a normal restriction feel arbitrary.

Make allowances discoverable and update the actual remaining count after failure. Policies may change with usage, but the current one should be described accurately.

An abstract best number cannot replace the conditions of a personal site. Cost, maintenance capacity and scope determine the choice. A default should be explainable and open to reassessment.

## Check neighbouring experiences

Longer answers affect scrolling and composer visibility. Stronger effects affect reading and reduced motion. Moving navigation into a menu affects discovery. A default often changes the experience around it.

Follow entering, reading, interacting, changing preferences and returning rather than judging one screenshot. A useful starting point should remain consistent through that journey.

Good defaults offer a trustworthy beginning while respecting subsequent choices. Together, these make settings part of the product rather than an isolated list of preferences.
